This page sets out the capabilities that are relevant to mine water and leaves it to you to judge the fit. When we have a mining deployment, it will be named here with its operating data.

Why these conditions suit it

Brackish water for mine sites is the design case, not an adaptation.

High-volume process water and concentrated wastewater streams with high TDS and hardness are what SonixED™ was built for. Four capabilities matter here.

01

Brine reduction mining operations can act on

Recovery of up to 98% in suitable applications, leaving 2–5% residual brine in selected cases against 25–50% for reverse osmosis. Where the site has no discharge route and evaporation ponds are the alternative, the residual fraction is the whole argument.

02

Scaling control without anti-scalant

Acoustic cavitation prevents scaling at nucleation, so hardness and silica do not dictate a dosing regime. Minimal chemical dosing and no anti-scalant, on water that would normally require both.

03

Selective removal, not total

The membrane coating sets which ions move. Where a downstream process needs certain ions retained, or a recovery stream needs them concentrated, selectivity is configurable per application rather than fixed.

04

Containerised, for sites without infrastructure

SalinBloc™ is a standardised modular platform for pilots, distributed deployment and staged validation, with the complete treatment stack, control systems and telemetry inside. Remote sites are its normal case.

Fit

Where it is likely to work, and where it is not.

Likely fit

Streams in the 1,500–20,000 ppm TDS range. High hardness or high silica. Sites with no discharge route, where disposal volume drives the cost. Remote operations where a containerised unit is easier than built infrastructure.

Not the primary use case

Acid mine drainage, where the problem is pH and dissolved metals rather than ionic load. Streams whose dominant contaminant is suspended solids or organics. Anything needing full demineralisation, which is a different process.
