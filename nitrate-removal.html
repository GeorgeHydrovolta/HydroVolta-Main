The obligation

Fifty milligrams per litre, already in force.

The EU nitrate limit 50 mg/l is not a future target. It is a parametric value a supplier is either meeting or not.

01

EU Drinking Water Directive

Directive (EU) 2020/2184 sets a parametric value of 50 mg/l for nitrate (NO₃¯) in water intended for human consumption. Member states transposed it by January 2023, so a supplier above the value is out of compliance now rather than ahead of a deadline.

02

WHO guideline value

The WHO Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality set the same 50 mg/l for nitrate as the nitrate ion. It is a short-term exposure guideline, set to protect bottle-fed infants rather than to address chronic exposure.

03

Nitrates Directive

Directive 91/676/EEC uses the same threshold to identify polluted groundwater and designate Nitrate Vulnerable Zones. It governs the source of the contamination; the Drinking Water Directive governs what reaches the tap.

Why reverse osmosis struggles here

A non-selective answer to a selective problem.

It removes what you wanted to keep

An RO membrane rejects ions by size and charge, not identity. Nitrate leaves, and so do the calcium and magnesium that give the water its mineral balance.

So you pay to put them back

Remineralisation is a second process step with its own capital cost, consumable supply chain and maintenance. A unit operation added to undo the one before it.

And the brine is the constraint

RO typically produces 25–50% brine. Inland, with no coastal or river discharge, disposal is often what ends the business case.

The mechanism

Selective removal, by charge and by coating.

SonixED™ is electrodialysis reversal with ultrasound-assisted scaling control and a selective membrane coating.

EDR

Ion transport, not bulk rejection

An electric potential drives selected ions through ion-exchange membranes and out of the product stream. Transport is driven by charge rather than pressure across a barrier, so the process acts on ions individually.

SEL

The coating sets the selectivity

The membrane coating determines which ions move. Nitrate, sodium, chloride and fluoride are removed; calcium and magnesium are retained, so no post-treatment remineralisation is required.

US

Ultrasound keeps it running

Acoustic cavitation at the membrane surface prevents scaling at nucleation. In the high-hardness groundwater common to nitrate-affected catchments, this is what lets the retained minerals stay in solution instead of fouling the stack.
