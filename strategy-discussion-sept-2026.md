## The Trigger: What George Observed in the Field

George noted that conversations with prospective clients revealed a framing problem with the current website. The site leads with technology and recovery percentages ("90%+ water recovery", "zero liquid discharge"). What clients actually respond to is **what they gain** — not what is discharged or wasted.

His example:
> "You withdraw 1,000L of difficult groundwater. Conventional treatment gives you 700L. With our system you use 950L. That's the story."

---

## Defining the Real Client Pain

### The Five Pains (Morocco Farm Case Study)

A farm operator near Marrakesh, Morocco — 5 hectares, saline groundwater — provided the clearest articulation of real client pain:

**Pain 1 — The silent loss happening right now**  
He is already farming with salty water. His fruit is already salt-stressed. He is losing yield, quality, and market price every season. This is not a future risk — it is a current, daily, quantifiable loss he has accepted because the alternative looked worse.

**Pain 2 — The sacrifice problem with conventional treatment**  
"We can't afford to lose 30m³ out of every 100m³ we pump." In a water-scarce region, this is existential. Conventional RO forced him to choose between two bad options: lose water volume, or keep the salinity problem. He chose to keep his water volume, even at the cost of crop quality.

**Pain 3 — The brine has nowhere to go**  
Inland site near Marrakesh. No river, no coast, no disposal infrastructure. Conventional treatment doesn't just cost him water — it produces a liability with no exit. This is why the "70m³ answer" is actually worse than it appears on paper.

**Pain 4 — The remineralisation trap**  
Treating to drinking quality strips everything. Then minerals must be added back for plants and humans. An extra cost step that makes no agronomic sense for irrigation. He needs the *right* water, not the *purest* water.

**Pain 5 — CAPEX/OPEX vs. the value actually at stake**  
Nobody has yet priced what salty fruit costs him per season — lower yield, lower grade, lower price per kg. That calculation changes the treatment economics entirely.

---

## The Core Reframe

**The farmer doesn't have a water treatment problem.**  
He has a farm productivity problem that water treatment can solve.

> *"His water chose his crops for him."*

Currently growing: olives and cumin — crops selected because they survive the salt, not because they are what he wants to grow or what the market pays best for.

What he would grow with treated water: **fruits, vegetables, edible crops** — higher value, higher revenue per hectare, a completely different farm.

**The real loss is not in litres. It's in the farm he should have.**

## The Strategic Tension

### The Risk of Agricultural Framing

If the homepage leads with a farmer and salty crops, a municipal utility director or industrial plant manager may self-select out — even though Hydrovolta is exactly the right solution for their problem.

Hydrovolta's pipeline spans:
- Agricultural operators (Morocco, similar regions)
- Municipal utilities
- Industrial facilities
- Remote communities

A sector-specific emotional hook risks excluding buyers from other sectors who face the same underlying pain with different surface symptoms.

---

## The Three Options

**Option 1 — Universal principle on homepage, sector stories on applications page**  
Homepage: *"You have a water source. Something about that water is limiting what you can do with it. That gap — between what's in the ground and what you can actually use — is what Hydrovolta closes."*  
Applications page: Morocco story + utility story + industrial story. Each sector recognises itself.

**Option 2 — Lead with the farmer, signal breadth immediately**  
Emotional hook via the farmer story. Within the first scroll, show the same logic applies to utilities, industrials, municipalities. Risk: serves neither audience fully.

**Option 3 — Abstract the pain into a universal statement**  
The farmer's core pain: *"I have a resource I can't fully use, and that ceiling costs me something real every day."* A utility director pumping from a brackish aquifer and serving 70% of required households feels exactly this. Language changes; pain is identical. Hardest to execute well.

---

## Agreed Direction (Pending Advisor Input)

1. **Homepage** — Universal argument. Not crops, not litres. The principle: you have a resource, your treatment decision limits what you can do with it, Hydrovolta closes that gap.

2. **Applications page** — Morocco story as a *diagnosed opportunity* (not a completed case study, since the project is prospective). Frame: "Assessed situation — Marrakesh region, Morocco." Walk through the diagnostic: current situation → forced crop choices → why conventional treatment failed → what 90%+ recovery changes → what the land could support.

3. **Technology page** — Unchanged. SonixED™, recovery rates, comparison tables, performance data stays where it is. Engineers find it when they look for it.

4. **The ask changes** — From "request an assessment" toward "find out what your water is costing your land / your operation." Diagnostic framing, not sales framing.

## The Morocco Case Study — Draft Structure

**Situation:** 5 hectares, Marrakesh region, Morocco. Saline groundwater. Currently farming without treatment — salt reaching crops directly.

**Forced choices:** Crop selection limited to salt-tolerant varieties (olives, cumin). Full land utilisation impossible. Conventional RO rejected: 30m³ per 100m³ pumped becomes unmanageable inland brine; water volume loss unacceptable in water-scarce region.

**The calculation nobody had done:** What does growing the wrong crop on stressed soil cost per season, versus what a pilot actually costs?

**The Hydrovolta answer:** 90%+ recovery = 90m³ usable from every 100m³ pumped. The 10m³ concentrate is manageable — not a disposal crisis. No remineralisation needed for irrigation. Crop choice opens up. The farm the operator wanted becomes possible.

**Note:** Farmer's name withheld. Referenced as "a farm operator near Marrakesh, Morocco."
